# Venture 3D Studio · student guide

1. **Explore.** Open the studio to see the editable CLAV compression jersey. Drag across the viewport to orbit it, or right-drag to pan. Scroll or pinch to zoom. Use the front, side or top camera preset to line up a part before moving it.
2. **Bring in your concept.** Use **Add reference** to choose a Mission 1 image and identify its view. Use **Project & import** to enter a short product description. A reference helps you decide how to shape the model. The browser builds from editable parts and templates.
3. **Customize.** Pick a component in the scene list or viewport. Use the move, rotate or scale control, or enter values in the inspector. Select a finish and color. For a logo, choose the component that should carry it and apply your image as its texture. Check the result from more than one angle. Undo restores the previous edit.
4. **Save two ways.** Choose **Save project** to keep a browser copy so you can continue after refreshing on this device. Choose **Download editable backup** as well. Keep the backup when moving between computers or clearing browser data. Choose **Open project or GLB** to resume editing from that file.
5. **Confirm.** Review the whole model and choose **Confirm Model**. After validation succeeds, download **Venture_3D_Confirmed_Handoff.zip**. Keep this ZIP intact for Mission 3. It contains the confirmed model, its preview and manifest, the editable snapshot and handoff instructions. Confirm again after later changes to create a fresh package.

## What to carry into Mission 3

Bring the most recent confirmed ZIP. Extract it when the next mission asks for the files. The website viewer uses `model.glb`. The manifest describes the confirmed design, dimensions and suggested display settings. Keep `project.json` to revise the design later. Confirming a design saves a handoff for you to use in Mission 3.

## About the CLAV starter

The shirt was modeled from the supplied concept during development. Its 19 editable components include the fabric body, sleeves, collar, raised lime clavicle pads, chest lettering and ventilation detail. The front drawing guides its form. Garment depth and dimensions are design estimates.
